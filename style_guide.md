## 1. Tone & Register

The narrative thrives on the juxtaposition between epic fantasy tropes (world-ending wars, legendary heroes, ancient artifacts) and mundane slice-of-life struggles (cleaning windows, chopping potatoes, getting paid). 

### Narrative Voice
*   **Deadpan & Pragmatic:** The protagonist (Daisy) is a former war hero now trapped in the body of a maid. Her inner narrative should be highly pragmatic, weary, and deadpan. Avoid overly emotional or flowery prose in her internal monologues; she views both magical assassins and dirty laundry as equally annoying chores.
*   **Comedic Contrast:** Emphasize the comedic timing of Daisy's overpowered (OP) reflexes interrupting mundane situations (e.g., casually slapping a baron across the room while saying, "I am holding back").

### Character-Specific Dialogue Registers
*   **Daisy (데이지):** Terse, blunt, and informal. When speaking to enemies or strangers, she drops all honorifics and uses the absolute minimum number of words (e.g., *“안 비웠는데”* → "It's not [empty]." / *“맛없어”* → "Tastes bad."). When forced to act like a maid, her polite speech should feel stiff, robotic, or begrudgingly short. She explicitly notes that speaking in long sentences feels exhausting and unfamiliar in her new body.
*   **The Head Maid (하녀장):** Professional, exasperated, and strictly polite. Use standard formal English. She acts as the "straight man" to Daisy's eccentricities. 
*   **Roux (루):** Sly, eloquent, and highly sarcastic. He speaks with formal politeness, but his word choices should feel slightly mocking and overly theatrical to contrast with Daisy's bluntness (e.g., *“하녀인 하녀 양”* → "Miss Maid who is a maid").
*   **The Assassin/Butler:** Stiff, militaristic, and excessively serious. He speaks like a subordinate giving a mission report, even when asking for laundry tips.
*   **Nobles & Thugs:** Arrogant, condescending, and bombastic. They should sound self-important to make Daisy’s immediate physical humbling of them funnier.

## 3. Formatting Rules

Korean web novels utilize specific punctuation brackets (e.g., < >, 『 』) to denote special terms, which must be adapted to standard English publishing conventions.

### Inner Monologues
*   **Rule:** Translate Korean single quotes (‘...’) used for thought into **Italics** without quotation marks. 
*   **Example:** 
    *   *Source:* ‘나는 분명 죽었는데?’
    *   *Translation:* *But I definitely died?*

### System Prompts, Skills, and Guilds
*   **Rule:** The source text uses angle brackets (`< >`) for guilds, special items, and spells. In English, use **Capitalization and Brackets `[ ]`** for items/skills to make them stand out, but just Capitalize guild names.
*   **Examples:**
    *   *Guilds:* <베리드 렛> → the Buried Rat (or the Buried Rat Guild).
    *   *Items/Spells:* <자비로운 한 입> → [Merciful Bite]
    *   *Organizations:* <마도 연합> → The Magic Coalition

### Written Text (Letters, Books, Notes)
*   **Rule:** The source text uses corner brackets (`『 』`) for reading material or notes. In English, use **Italics and Title Case** for book titles, and **Blockquotes** for the contents of letters or written notes.
*   **Example (Book):** 
    *   *Source:* 『말을 곱게 하는 기술』
    *   *Translation:* She handed me a book titled *The Art of Speaking Politely*.
*   **Example (Notes/Letters):** Format Daisy's bullet-point interrogation note to Roux as an indented block or blockquote to separate it visually from the narrative.

### Paragraph Structure and Pacing
*   **Rule:** Korean web novels favor single-sentence paragraphs for dramatic effect and scrolling ease. While you should combine closely related sentences into standard English paragraphs to avoid a disjointed read, **preserve line breaks for comedic beats and action impacts**. 
*   **Example:** Keep Daisy's one-word responses on their own line to emphasize her deadpan delivery.

## 4. Pronoun and Perspective Guidelines

### First-Person Limited ("I")
The story is strictly told from Daisy's first-person perspective. The narrative distance should feel incredibly close, as she frequently filters the world through her past experiences as a commander.

### Gender Transition & Pronoun Consistency
*   **The Reincarnation Quirk:** The protagonist was originally a female who used ancient magic to turn her body into a male (her dead brother, Andert) for 10 years during the war. She has now been resurrected in a female body (belonging to her dead friend, Bertie) but renamed herself Daisy. 
*   **Internal Consistency:** Daisy refers to her past male body as exactly that—a male body she wore like a tool. When referring to her time as Andert, she acknowledges living as a man. 
*   **Pronoun Rule:** Use female pronouns (she/her) when referring to her current state as Daisy. When she references her 10 years in the war, she should sound detached regarding her gender, treating her current female body as something she has to "squeeze back into."
*   **Example Context:** *"For ten years as Andert Farger... and as a man, the body of a woman felt like it belonged to someone else."* Maintain this sense of physical detachment and dysphoria in the translation; it is central to her characterization.
